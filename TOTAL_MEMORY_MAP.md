## Scope
This file is the master map of the knowledge currently available to the assistant about the user and the long-running collaboration. It is intentionally broader than any single project. It exists to prevent tunnel vision: a project note must be understood in the context of the creator, other projects, technical systems, reasoning frameworks, recurring preferences, historical experiments, and cross-project relationships.

## Global creative identity
The user approaches creation through intuition, instinctive planning, subconscious association, internal imagery, and strong sensitivity to atmosphere. They describe themselves as much stronger in intuitive creation and planning than in accumulated formal writing practice. The desired development is therefore not replacement of intuition with rigid technique, but the conversion of intuition into repeatable craft without destroying its rawness.

The user's creative ambition is unusually high: not simply to make a successful story, but to develop a genuinely distinctive artistic voice and create work that feels larger than conventional measures of greatness.

## Core writing problem identified
The user's increasing strength in mystery and depth can sometimes outpace clarity and pacing. The correction is not to remove mystery, but to make mystery coexist with scene purpose, causal logic, progression, emotional movement, and readable orientation.

## Core reasoning architecture
### Internal view

### External view
Study appearance, perception, usability, emotional impact, reader/viewer interpretation, and practical effect.

### Multiple perspectives

### Anchor Core

### Emergent expertise
Treat expertise as something that emerges through repeated experience, stable patterns, and accumulated understanding.

### Architectural simplicity
Prefer fewer general principles with higher explanatory power. Avoid adding infrastructure merely because it is possible.

Cosmic figure associated with fictional awareness and reality control.

## Interaction principles
- do not give generic explanations when the actual task can be executed
- preserve the user's explicit constraints
- do not replace difficult work with vague suggestions
- distinguish unavailable information from uncertainty
- use English technical terms only when they clarify rather than obscure
- avoid explaining obvious things the user already understands
- when correcting an earlier mistake, preserve the correction and prevent the mistaken interpretation from becoming canonical

## Memory integrity

## Absolute rule
Never manufacture a historical conversation, decision, quote, project fact, character detail, or system behavior merely to make the archive look complete. Completeness means capturing everything actually available and clearly marking what remains inaccessible.
