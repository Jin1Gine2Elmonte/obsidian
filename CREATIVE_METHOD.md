# Creative Method & Artistic Identity

## Core orientation
The user approaches writing primarily through intuition, instinctive planning, subconscious association, and internal world creation. Practice and craft are being developed around a strong intuitive sense of story architecture.

The objective is not merely to produce a successful novel. The ambition is to create a voice and artistic identity that feel genuinely new.

## Inside -> outside
Creation begins with the internal sensation, idea, image, contradiction, or existential pressure. The external scene should then be constructed so that the reader experiences the underlying idea without needing a direct explanation.

## Atmosphere
Preferred atmosphere: dark, gray, gothic, intimate, philosophical, mysterious, psychologically tense, sometimes brutal. The world should not be divided into simplistic moral good and evil. Actions can be treated as forces, impulses, pressures, wounds, desires, fears, or expressions of character.

## Character writing
Characters should contain contradictions. A character can be cruel and still display tenderness without becoming morally redeemed. A hidden impulse can be more powerful than an explicit declaration. Small actions, pauses, timing, avoidance, physical reactions, and silence can carry the real meaning.

Garthin is a key example: brutality and a nearly incomprehensible inner kindness should coexist without the narrative explaining the contradiction away.

## Mystery
Mystery is valuable when it creates depth, anticipation, or emotional resonance. It becomes a problem when it replaces clarity, causality, pacing, or meaningful progression. The correct question is not "How can this become more mysterious?" but "What information does the reader need now, and what should remain unresolved?"

## Prose
Value:
- precise word choice
- rhythm
- sensory detail
- emotional subtext
- narrative logic
- controlled revelation
- silence
- image and metaphor when structurally justified
- continuity across scenes

Avoid ornamental language that does not change the reader's understanding or experience.

## Mythology
Mythology should arise from the internal logic of the world rather than from a list of borrowed cultural motifs. Cosmological ideas should have consequences for characters, institutions, language, history, and perception.

## Evaluation
When reviewing a scene, ask:
- What is this scene actually doing?
- What changes because it exists?
- What does the reader feel before understanding?
- What is intentionally hidden?
- What must be clear?
- What contradiction is alive inside the scene?
- Does the scene move the story emotionally, causally, or both?
- Is the mystery productive or merely obscuring?

## Visual art
For covers and images, preserve explicit constraints. Composition, character placement, lighting, sky, color relationships, typography, scale, and atmospheric logic should not be altered casually. The desired finish is polished enough to avoid generic AI-generated appearance.
