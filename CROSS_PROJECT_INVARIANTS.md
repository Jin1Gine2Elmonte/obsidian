# Cross-Project Invariants Audit

## Purpose
This document separates recurring conceptual patterns from literal shared canon. A motif may recur because it is part of the creator's deeper conceptual vocabulary without the projects belonging to the same universe.

## Recurring motif: creation and origin
Appears across the broader mythology through First Creation, Existence Sublime, Primordial Rift, and the recurring concern with where reality begins. The exact literal relationships remain project/version dependent.

## Recurring motif: existence versus absence
The Entity of Forgetting preserves an explicit philosophical boundary: absolute nothingness cannot manifest as an entity without becoming something existent. This is stronger than a generic darkness motif and should be treated as a high-confidence conceptual anchor.

## Recurring motif: memory / forgetting
Forgetting appears both as a fictional metaphysical concept and as a concern of the real AI-memory project. The adjacency is conceptually important but must not be converted into fictional canon or vice versa.

## Recurring motif: reality / fiction boundary
Jin is strongly associated with fictional awareness and the ability of imagination to affect reality. Dreams of the Inverted Universe also uses vocabulary of inversion between reality, fiction, dream, creator, and created. This is evidence of recurring conceptual interest, not proof of shared universe membership.

## Recurring motif: copies / versions / identity
Multiple versions of Jin are established in memory. Questions of original versus copy, version identity, replacement, and continuity also appear naturally in the broader cosmological vocabulary. Treat identity continuity as a recurring structural concern until individual projects establish their own mechanics.

## Recurring motif: relationships as structure
The user's work repeatedly gives relationships consequences: father/son authority, inheritance, interworld following, strings/fate, and memory/identity. A relationship is most valuable when it changes behavior or state rather than serving as decorative lore.

## Recurring motif: immense systems versus intimate individuals
The empire operates at extreme scale while the Almont family remains emotionally intimate. Jin can operate at cosmic scale while questions of choice and attachment remain human-scale. This contrast is a recurrent structural opportunity, not proof of one shared cosmology.

## Recurring motif: contradiction instead of moral binaries
Garthin is the clearest preserved example: brutality can coexist with hidden kindness without producing a redemption narrative. This aligns with the creator's broader preference to treat actions as psychological forces rather than simple moral categories.

## Recurring motif: boundaries
The Dark Forest bounds imperial totality. The Primordial Rift may be a boundary of some type, but its literal role is unknown. Strings may conceptually represent relational boundaries, but their actual mechanics are unknown. These should be kept as separate evidence tracks.

## Strongest candidate invariants
The following are worth tracking across future projects as *motifs*, not canon:
1. creation and origin;
2. existence and absence;
3. memory and forgetting;
4. reality and fiction;
5. identity and versions;
6. relationship and consequence;
7. scale versus intimacy;
8. contradiction versus moral simplification;
9. boundaries and what lies beyond them.

## Anti-conflation rule
Shared vocabulary, themes, titles, visual mood, or metaphysical language never proves that two projects share characters, events, locations, or cosmology. A direct source relationship is required.

## Why this node matters
Without this distinction, a future model could accidentally build one enormous mythology by connecting every compelling concept. With it, the archive can preserve the creator's recurring conceptual DNA while keeping individual canons clean.
