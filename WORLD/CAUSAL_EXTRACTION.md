# World Causal Extraction

This is a cross-character causal map built only from durable remembered elements. It deliberately separates observed history from analytical consequences.

## Chain A — Almont family
Known sequence: Almont is emperor -> Arisa is his wife -> Ryota is his son -> Arisa's death is witnessed in the remembered child-history -> Ryota later lives in sustained rebellion against Almont -> Ryota leaves/disappears into other lands -> Ryota eventually returns because his position/future influence matters.

### Why this chain matters
The event is not merely family tragedy. It can connect trauma to succession, identity, political inheritance, resistance, and the tension between private escape and public obligation. These are analytical consequences; the exact causal links require scene recovery.

## Chain B — Empire / Dark Forest
Known: the empire controls most of the world -> the Dark Forest remains outside effective imperial control.

### Why this chain matters
The empire therefore has a structural limit. That limit can be geographic, military, epistemic, or all three, but the exact form must be recovered rather than assumed.

## Chain C — Power / blood / politics
Known: Powers are comparatively logical and constrained -> Powers can be inherited through blood -> a spiritual-vessel concept exists.

### Possible systemic consequence
Blood inheritance can connect ability to family identity, social rank, military value, marriage, succession, and fear of rival bloodlines. These are consequences to test against the actual worldbuilding.

## Chain D — Jin / worlds
Known: Jin is associated with imagination, fictional awareness, extraordinary reality control, and a world/realm premise that allows imagination to affect humans. Adam travels between worlds and follows Jin.

### Why this chain matters
Adam creates a possible character-level bridge between Jin's extreme cosmological status and the experience of moving between worlds. Historical scenes should determine whether that bridge is actually central.

## Chain E — Versions of Jin
Known: multiple versions/iterations have been discussed, but the accessible context lacks a complete catalog.

### Critical danger
A later model could accidentally merge all Jin descriptions into one super-character. That would produce a false canon in which traits belonging to different project generations are attributed to one entity.

### Required extraction
Every historical Jin statement should first be assigned a version/world/phase when possible, and only then considered for cross-version invariants.

## Chain F — Garthin
Known: Garthin is a ruler characterized by brutality combined with a hidden spark of kindness, and that contradiction should be shown through behavior.

### Why this chain matters
Garthin is an example of the user's broader character philosophy: the most revealing information may be an action that contradicts the public identity of the character.

## Scene-level extraction principle
For each recovered scene, record:
- initial state;
- pressure/trigger;
- action;
- immediate consequence;
- character revelation;
- world consequence;
- information revealed;
- information deliberately hidden;
- later consequences;
- whether the scene changes canon.

This is more valuable than a list of character traits because it preserves causality.
