# Almont — Memory-Density Extraction

## Status
High-confidence recurring character anchor, but the accessible context is fragmented. This note records durable elements without inventing missing scenes.

## Core remembered identity
Emperor Almont (المونت) is a tyrannical ruler and the center of a major imperial/family branch. His significance is not only political: his relationship with Arisa and Ryota forms a human-scale pressure system of authority, inheritance, rebellion, trauma, and emotional deprivation.

## Family structure
- Almont — emperor / father / ruler.
- Arisa — wife and mother figure in the family branch.
- Ryota — son whose later history is marked by rebellion and estrangement.
- Benjamin — adviser associated with Almont's rule.

## Arisa
Arisa's death is a major remembered event in the family history. The accessible context specifically preserves the fact that the child/young-character history involves witnessing her death. The exact circumstances, perpetrator, location, and immediate aftermath are not sufficiently preserved and must remain open.

## Father / son architecture
The Almont–Ryota relationship is remembered as harsh rather than warm. Ryota repeatedly rebels against his father, leaves the capital, disappears into other lands, and later returns because his position and future influence matter.

This creates a structural distinction between:
- inheritance as political power;
- inheritance as burden;
- fatherhood as authority;
- identity as resistance to the father's design.

Those are analytical dimensions, not automatic canon beyond the remembered history.

## Empire and family are coupled
Almont's private family dynamics are not separable from his political authority. The same system that makes him powerful as emperor also shapes the emotional environment around his child. The archive should therefore cross-link family events with imperial decisions whenever historical evidence exists.

## Recovery targets
1. Exact description of Almont's personality and worldview.
2. His feelings toward Arisa.
3. His feelings toward Ryota.
4. What he expected Ryota to become.
5. What triggered Ryota's rebellion.
6. Exact events around Arisa's death.
7. How the court responded.
8. Benjamin's role in Almont's decisions.
9. How the empire's scale affected Almont's psychology.
10. Whether Almont is purely tyrannical or contains meaningful contradiction in later scenes.

## Canon protection
Do not flatten Almont into "generic tyrant". Historical dialogue and scenes should determine whether his cruelty is ideological, psychological, strategic, habitual, inherited, defensive, or some mixture.
