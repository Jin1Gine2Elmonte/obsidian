# Scene Evidence Protocol

## Purpose
The strongest missing layer in the current archive is scene-level evidence. Character summaries tell us what is remembered; scenes can tell us why it is true, how it changed, and what the user actually established.

## Every recovered scene should capture
- conversation date if available;
- project/version;
- scene title or identifying phrase;
- characters present;
- location;
- time/age;
- immediate objective;
- explicit facts;
- actions;
- dialogue with high-value wording preserved;
- emotional state;
- power usage;
- revelation;
- irreversible change;
- relationship change;
- consequences;
- canonical status;
- links to affected nodes.

## Evidence hierarchy
1. Exact user-written text or explicit user statement.
2. Exact correction or later clarification by the user.
3. Repeated consistent statements across conversations.
4. Assistant synthesis that clearly cites its basis.
5. Analytical inference.
6. Creative extrapolation.

Only levels 1–3 should normally create or revise core canon without review.

## Scene beats to prioritize
### Jin
- first awareness of fictional reality;
- moments of reality control;
- refusal to intervene;
- encounters with another version;
- moments of emotional limitation;
- decisions that affect worlds.

### Almont / Ryota / Arisa
- family life before rupture;
- Arisa's death;
- immediate aftermath;
- escape;
- first return;
- father-son confrontation;
- later departure/return cycles;
- succession/political discussions.

### Garthin
- first appearance;
- public acts of brutality;
- hidden kindness;
- contradiction witnessed by another character;
- moments where authority conflicts with attachment.

### Adam
- first meeting with Jin;
- first world transition;
- disagreement with Jin;
- evidence of independent motive;
- encounters with different Jin versions.

### Power system
- first explanation of Magic;
- first explanation of Powers;
- inheritance revelation;
- spiritual-vessel mechanics;
- explicit limitations;
- failed uses;
- political consequences.

## Rule against summary contamination
If a summary conflicts with a recovered scene, the scene wins unless a later user correction explicitly changes the canon.

## Why this matters
A world becomes trustworthy when its laws, personalities, and history are supported by concrete moments rather than only by abstractions. The archive should therefore become increasingly scene-dense as historical material becomes available.
