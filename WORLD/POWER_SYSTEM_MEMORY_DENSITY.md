# Power System — Memory-Density Extraction

## Core remembered distinction
The world distinguishes between two major power categories.

### Magic
Magic is transcendent, difficult to learn, and can operate beyond ordinary logic.

### Powers
Powers are more logical and constrained. They can be inherited through blood, making lineage a direct connection between ability, family, identity, and politics.

### Spiritual vessel
A spiritual-vessel concept exists within the remembered architecture. Exact mechanics are not preserved sufficiently to define a complete system.

## Structural consequences
The distinction creates different sources of inequality: knowledge/learning, blood/inheritance, and capacity/vessel. It can therefore influence class, military organization, family prestige, succession, fear, and political legitimacy.

## Canon boundary
The current context does not reliably preserve exact stages, terminology, limits, energy sources, costs, compatibility rules, training, failure states, interaction between Magic and Powers, or detailed spiritual-vessel mechanics. These must be recovered, not invented as canon.

## Recovery targets
Search historical material for comparisons, training scenes, fights, explanations, inheritance scenes, vessel failures, and explicit statements about what each category cannot do. Negative rules are especially valuable because they prevent arbitrary escalation.

## World connection
Power is not merely a combat mechanic. Blood inheritance can feed directly into Almont's family politics, imperial military organization, and the identities of characters who resist inherited expectations.
