# Capital / Court — Recovered Worldbuilding Surface

## Why the capital matters
The capital is not merely a location. It is repeatedly remembered as the place to which the family returns, where Almont's authority is concentrated, and from which Ryota/Jin can leave and later return.

## Structural roles
- seat of imperial authority;
- family pressure chamber;
- political network center;
- place where future influence becomes consequential;
- symbolic home that can simultaneously represent confinement.

## Return pattern
A key remembered tension is that return to the capital does not automatically mean emotional reconciliation. A character can return because politics, position, influence, duty, or necessity requires it while remaining internally opposed to the sovereign father.

## Court questions to recover
- imperial palace layout;
- institutions and councils;
- military command;
- adviser structure;
- succession ceremonies;
- noble/family factions;
- public versus private behavior of Almont;
- where Arisa lived and how visible she was politically;
- Benjamin's access to power;
- how Ryota is treated publicly versus privately;
- how the capital reacts to repeated disappearances and returns.

## Narrative use
The capital should be treated as a node where multiple systems intersect: family, government, military, reputation, succession, and hidden information. Scenes there should ideally move more than one system at once.
