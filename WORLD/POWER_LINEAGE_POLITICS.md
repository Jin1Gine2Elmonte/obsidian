# Blood Powers ↔ Lineage ↔ Imperial Politics

## Recovered premise
Powers are remembered as more logical and constrained than Magic and can be inherited through blood. This makes ability potentially hereditary rather than purely merit-based.

## Political consequences to investigate
A hereditary power system can affect:
- legitimacy of rulers;
- marriage strategy;
- succession;
- military caste formation;
- fear of bloodlines;
- protection or persecution of gifted families;
- claims of divine or biological right;
- rebellions against inherited status.

These are structural consequences, not established events.

## Almont branch
Because Almont's family is politically central, the archive should test whether blood inheritance affects:
- why Ryota matters politically;
- what Almont expects from his son;
- whether succession is merely political or also power-based;
- whether Arisa's lineage matters;
- whether Benjamin's advice concerns power as well as governance.

No direct link is currently established.

## Jin problem
If the child in the Almont branch is later confirmed to be a version of Jin, blood inheritance could become a bridge between Jin's cosmic identity and the imperial world. If Jin and Ryota are distinct, this connection must remain localized to Ryota and the imperial family.

## Magic contrast
Magic is remembered as beyond ordinary logic and difficult to learn. This creates a philosophical contrast:

**Powers -> inheritance / structure / lineage**

**Magic -> transcendence / learning / exception**

The narrative value could emerge from conflict between systems, but exact mechanics and social attitudes require source recovery.

## Archive target
Future scenes involving combat, training, succession, family discussion, military recruitment, or political marriage should be tagged for explicit power-lineage information.
