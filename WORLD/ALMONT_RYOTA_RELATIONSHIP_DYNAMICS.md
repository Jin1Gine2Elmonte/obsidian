# Almont ↔ Ryota — Relationship Dynamics

## Recovered pattern
The relationship is remembered not as a single conflict but as a repeated state cycle:

**authority -> resistance -> separation -> temporary distance -> return -> renewed pressure**

This cycle matters because it means the relationship changes through repetition. Each return carries the accumulated history of previous departures.

## Two simultaneous relationships
Almont is both:
- father;
- emperor.

Ryota is both:
- son;
- politically significant heir/figure.

A confrontation can therefore contain two incompatible conversations at once:
- a father addressing a child;
- a sovereign addressing a politically consequential person.

## Emotional asymmetry
The accessible memory describes the relationship as lacking ordinary familial warmth. It does not establish whether either party lacks attachment; coldness and absence of warmth are not proof of absence of attachment.

Recovery should specifically distinguish:
- affection;
- possession;
- expectation;
- fear;
- pride;
- control;
- disappointment;
- dependency;
- political necessity.

## Why repeated departures matter
Repeated departure is not merely transportation. It can function as a measurable behavioral response to the capital's pressure. But every departure also changes the political meaning of the return.

A future scene should be tagged for:
- trigger of departure;
- what was said before leaving;
- who permitted or prevented it;
- route/status of departure;
- what changed while absent;
- why return became necessary;
- how Almont interpreted the return;
- whether Ryota returned voluntarily.

## Arisa as the missing center
Because Arisa's death is remembered as the pivotal rupture, her presence before the death is essential to reconstructing the father/son relationship accurately. Without her earlier role, the archive risks explaining the relationship only through trauma and ignoring the family state that existed before it.

## Canon warning
The file is a relational model, not a claim that every listed emotional category is established. Historical scenes must determine which forces were actually present.
