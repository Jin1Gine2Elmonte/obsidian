# Jin — Human-Scale Interface

## Purpose
Jin's cosmic status risks making every human-scale storyline irrelevant. This node tracks the bridge between extraordinary ontological agency and ordinary emotional stakes.

## Known foundation
Jin is associated with fictional awareness, imagination as an effective realm, and extraordinary reality control. That gives him potential access to solutions unavailable to ordinary characters.

## Narrative pressure
The important recovery question is therefore not simply "what can Jin do?" but:
- what does he choose not to change?
- what does he not understand despite his power?
- what emotional consequences cannot be solved by reality manipulation?
- what relationships retain meaning when power asymmetry becomes extreme?
- what happens when another character has information Jin lacks?
- what does Jin value enough to leave untouched?

These are analytical questions, not assertions of canon.

## Human-scale anchors to recover
The most valuable scenes are likely those in which Jin interacts with:
- family;
- a child/younger self;
- Almont and the imperial world;
- Adam;
- ordinary people who do not understand his scale;
- a rival or force he cannot simply erase;
- a version of himself that disagrees with him.

## Why this matters
A cosmic protagonist becomes dramatically meaningful when the reader can observe a choice whose cost is not measured in destructive power. Historical conversation recovery should therefore prioritize scenes of refusal, attachment, misunderstanding, regret, protection, conflict, and consequences.

## Canon safety
Nothing in this file should be promoted to canon unless a recovered conversation or artifact demonstrates it. Its function is to direct retrieval toward the scenes most likely to contain the actual character architecture.
