# Scene Recovery Method — Jin World

## Why scenes matter
The user's strongest character and worldbuilding material is often encoded in events, behavior, dialogue, and consequences rather than in abstract summaries. A recovered scene can establish multiple nodes simultaneously.

## For every recovered scene
Record:
- date/source;
- project/version;
- location;
- characters present;
- immediate objective;
- hidden objective when explicitly established;
- what each character knows;
- what each character believes incorrectly;
- action;
- reaction;
- revelation;
- reversal;
- consequence;
- canon changes produced by the scene.

## High-value scene types
Prioritize scenes involving:
- Jin and another version of Jin;
- Jin and Almont;
- Ryota and Almont;
- Arisa before/around death;
- Garthin under extreme pressure;
- Adam meeting Jin;
- capital departures/returns;
- Dark Forest encounters;
- demonstrations of Magic or inherited Powers;
- revelations about cosmology.

## Why dialogue must be preserved
A summary can tell us that a father and son were estranged. Original dialogue can reveal whether the estrangement is hatred, fear, disappointment, longing, political defiance, humiliation, or an unstable mixture. Preserve exact dialogue whenever source transcripts are available.

## Scene-to-system extraction
After archiving the scene, update:
1. character nodes;
2. relationship graph;
3. timeline;
4. world rules;
5. canon/provenance;
6. open questions;
7. project/version state.

## Anti-loss rule
Never reduce a pivotal scene to one sentence if its dialogue or behavioral detail is the evidence that explains later character behavior.
