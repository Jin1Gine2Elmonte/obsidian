# Empire — Power vs Administrative Reality

## Why this node exists
The empire is remembered at an enormous scale: most of the world under one authority, roughly 1,060 military cities, and some cities with populations reaching hundreds of millions in the world's established scale.

The useful question is therefore not only "how large is the empire?" but "what does that size force the world to be like?"

## Sovereignty is layered
A world-spanning empire cannot exercise the same kind of control everywhere. Future recovered canon should distinguish:
- direct control;
- delegated control;
- military occupation;
- tributary dependence;
- nominal sovereignty;
- contested territory;
- territory outside effective knowledge/control.

These categories are analytical until confirmed by the source material.

## The numbered military cities
The remembered numbering suggests a deliberate imperial classification system. The numbers could encode administrative order, military district, foundation sequence, strategic rank, or another system. Do not select one without evidence.

For each recovered city, preserve:
- number/name;
- location;
- function;
- population;
- commander/authority;
- resources;
- route to capital;
- neighboring cities;
- relation to Dark Forest;
- major historical events.

## Communication problem
At this scale, the story needs an answer to how orders, intelligence, taxes, armies, and emergencies cross enormous distances. This does not require realistic physics, but it does require internal consistency with the world's established technology/magic.

Possible mechanisms to investigate from canon:
- magical communication;
- courier networks;
- relay institutions;
- inherited Powers;
- specialized military infrastructure;
- extraordinary transport.

Do not invent a mechanism merely to solve the problem.

## Almont's true reach
Almont is remembered as emperor and tyrant. The archival model should distinguish:

**formal authority** — what the throne claims;

**operational authority** — what orders can actually be executed;

**personal authority** — what follows because of Almont himself;

**institutional authority** — what continues even without his presence.

This distinction may become important for understanding why a politically important son can repeatedly leave the capital while the empire still remains functional.

## Capital as a gravity well
The capital is not only a location. It is the point where succession, reputation, court politics, family pressure, and imperial legitimacy converge. A character can flee physically while remaining politically tied to it.

## Dark Forest as counterexample
The Dark Forest is remembered as outside effective imperial control. This is valuable because it gives the empire a measurable limit. A claim of near-total sovereignty becomes dramatically meaningful only because one region resists it.

## Canon boundary
All institutional conclusions above are consistency models, not hidden facts. Historical conversations should determine the actual administrative and magical mechanisms.
