# Dark Forest — Imperial Boundary

## Recovered state
The Dark Forest is remembered as a dangerous, mysterious region not controlled by the empire. The empire otherwise controls most of the world in the established setting.

## Why the boundary is structurally valuable
The forest is the clearest remembered counterexample to absolute imperial control. It therefore creates a boundary in at least three dimensions:

- **geographic:** territory outside normal administration;
- **political:** imperial authority cannot simply be assumed;
- **epistemic:** the unknown remains outside the state's complete knowledge.

These dimensions should be tested against source scenes rather than assumed identical.

## Questions to recover
- Why can the empire not control the forest?
- Is the barrier physical, magical, ecological, military, cultural, or all of these?
- Does the empire attempt repeated expeditions?
- Are military cities positioned near it?
- Does the capital possess reliable maps?
- Are there settlements or civilizations inside?
- Does the forest interact with the power system?
- Does it conceal historical information connected to Jin or the cosmology?

## Character relevance
The Dark Forest can change the meaning of a character's departure from the capital. Traveling into ordinary imperial territory is politically different from crossing into territory where imperial assumptions fail.

This should be recovered carefully in any scene involving Jin/Ryota, Adam, Garthin, or other characters.

## Canon boundary
Only the empire's inability to control the Dark Forest is currently retained as established. Causes, inhabitants, powers, and hidden history remain unresolved.
