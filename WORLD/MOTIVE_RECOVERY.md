# Motive Recovery — Jin / Almont World

## Why motives are missing from summaries
The accessible memory often preserves actions and outcomes but not the internal reasons that produced them. That creates a dangerous gap: later AI systems can mistake a plausible motive for remembered canon.

## Jin
Recover separately per variant:
- what he wants;
- what he fears;
- what he refuses;
- what he knows;
- what he believes about choice;
- what he believes about fiction/reality;
- what he protects;
- what he regrets;
- what he cannot change;
- what motivates world travel or intervention.

## Almont
Recover:
- conception of sovereignty;
- conception of fatherhood;
- desired form of obedience;
- fear of losing power;
- view of succession;
- view of Ryota;
- attachment to Arisa;
- meaning of empire;
- rationale behind punishments and decisions.

## Ryota / possible child-Jin branch
Recover:
- meaning of escape;
- reason for returning;
- desired distance from Almont;
- relationship to inheritance;
- relationship to power;
- whether rebellion is rejection, survival, identity formation, or another motive;
- what the character still wants from the father despite rebellion.

## Arisa
Recover her motives before her death, not merely her role in other characters' stories.

## Benjamin
Recover what he wants from advising Almont, what he fears, what he knows, and whether his public loyalty matches his private motive.

## Garthin
Recover the motive beneath both brutality and hidden kindness. The contradiction becomes powerful only when both behaviors emerge from a coherent internal organism rather than from arbitrary authorial reversal.

## Adam
Recover why he follows Jin, what would make him stop, what he wants independently, and whether his interworld travel serves Jin's goals or his own.

## Rule
A motive should be linked to an observable decision or repeated behavior whenever possible. Never convert a psychological interpretation into canon merely because it explains the character elegantly.
