# World State Transitions — Reconstructing How the Setting Changes

## Purpose
A lore archive often records states without recording the transitions that produced them. This file tracks the major remembered transitions that give the Jin/Almont world historical shape.

## Transition T1 — Family before rupture -> Arisa's death
Known destination: the family is permanently changed by Arisa's death. Exact prior state and exact event remain under-recovered.

Required extraction:
- family dynamics before the event;
- Arisa's own role;
- child's age and understanding;
- who caused the death;
- Almont's reaction;
- political consequences.

## Transition T2 — Arisa's death -> escape
Known: the child/son leaves or escapes after the traumatic event. Whether the escape is spontaneous, assisted, forced, or strategically planned is unknown.

## Transition T3 — escape -> return
Known: permanent disappearance does not occur. The character later returns to the capital because position/future influence makes the capital politically consequential.

Important distinction: return is not equivalent to reconciliation.

## Transition T4 — return -> sustained rebellion
Known: the father-son relationship remains harsh and rebellious rather than becoming conventionally warm.

## Transition T5 — rebellion -> repeated departures
Known: the character repeatedly leaves/disappears into other lands. Causes may differ across episodes.

## Transition T6 — imperial totality -> Dark Forest exception
Known: the empire controls most of the world while the Dark Forest remains outside effective control. This creates a permanent boundary to imperial totality.

## Transition T7 — power categories -> political consequences
Known: Magic and inherited Powers are distinct, and blood inheritance exists. The historical archive should recover where this begins to alter family, military, and state behavior.

## Transition T8 — one Jin representation -> another
Known: multiple Jin iterations/versions exist. Exact transition types are unknown. A rewrite may be historical rather than in-world chronology.

## Transition T9 — cosmic agency -> human-scale consequence
This is an analytical recovery target: determine the scenes where Jin's unusual ontological status produces concrete human consequences rather than merely cosmological spectacle.

## Rule
For every transition, the archive should record:
- previous state;
- trigger;
- actors;
- information available at the time;
- action;
- immediate consequence;
- delayed consequence;
- relationship changes;
- world-rule changes;
- source;
- current canon status.
