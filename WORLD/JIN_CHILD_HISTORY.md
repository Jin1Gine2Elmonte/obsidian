# Jin — Child / Capital / Rebellion History Thread

## Provenance
This thread comes from durable remembered worldbuilding rather than a complete transcript. It is important enough to isolate because it intersects Jin, Almont, Arisa, the capital, inheritance, rebellion, and later influence.

## Recovered sequence
1. A child/young **Jin** witnesses the killing/death of Arisa.
2. He escapes/flees after the traumatic event.
3. He does not remain permanently absent.
4. He later returns to the capital because the scale of his position and his future ability to influence others makes the capital politically consequential.
5. He lives a prolonged period of rebellion against his father.
6. The relationship with his father is harsh and lacks ordinary familial tenderness.
7. He repeatedly leaves the capital and disappears or travels into other territories through official or unofficial routes.

## Why this matters
The pattern is not merely "traumatized child becomes rebellious." It creates a long temporal arc in which:
- trauma leads to flight;
- political reality eventually pulls him back;
- return does not automatically create reconciliation;
- power/status can coexist with familial estrangement;
- geographic movement can function as psychological refusal;
- the capital can act simultaneously as home, authority, history, and source of conflict.

These are structural readings of the recovered sequence, not replacements for the missing scenes.
