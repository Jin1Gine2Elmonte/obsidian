# Jin — Child / Capital / Rebellion History Thread

## Provenance
This thread comes from durable remembered worldbuilding rather than a complete transcript. It is important enough to isolate because it intersects Jin, Almont, Arisa, Ryota, the capital, inheritance, and later influence.

## Recovered sequence
1. A child/young Jin witnesses the killing/death of Arisa.
2. He escapes/flees after the traumatic event.
3. He does not remain permanently absent.
4. He later returns to the capital because the scale of his position and his future ability to influence others makes the capital politically consequential.
5. He lives a prolonged period of rebellion against his father.
6. The relationship with his father is harsh and lacks ordinary familial tenderness.
7. He repeatedly leaves the capital and disappears or travels into other territories through official or unofficial routes.

## Why this matters
The pattern is not merely "traumatized child becomes rebellious." It creates a long temporal arc in which:
- trauma leads to flight;
- political reality eventually pulls him back;
- return does not create reconciliation;
- power/status can coexist with familial estrangement;
- geographic movement becomes an expression of psychological refusal;
- the capital becomes both home and source of conflict.

## Important distinction
The accessible context contains both the names Jin/Gin/Gene and the Almont/Ryota family branch in different summaries. Because the user has also spoken of multiple versions of Jin, do not automatically assume every detail in this thread belongs to the same final Jin incarnation. Use version tags when historical transcript evidence becomes available.

## Open chronology
The exact age at every stage, interval between Arisa's death and return, method of escape, reason for each later departure, and final state of the father-son relationship are not fully preserved.

## Recovery priority
This thread should be searched for exact scenes/dialogue because its true value is likely in the emotional transitions: what was seen, what was understood by the child, what was not understood, what triggered return, and what the father did across the repeated cycles of departure and return.
