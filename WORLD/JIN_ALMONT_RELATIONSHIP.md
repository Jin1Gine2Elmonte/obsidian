# Jin / Almont — Father, Sovereign, and Counterforce

## Recovered structure
The accessible memory places a character identified in the family branch as a child/young Jin in prolonged conflict with a tyrannical father, Almont. In related summaries the son is also described as Ryota, which is exactly why version/project disambiguation is required.

## Shared high-level pattern
Regardless of naming/version ambiguity, the remembered structure contains:
- imperial father/authority;
- child/son positioned inside that authority;
- maternal rupture through Arisa's death;
- escape;
- later return because external position/influence makes total disappearance impossible;
- long rebellion rather than simple reconciliation;
- repeated departures;
- emotional distance and lack of conventional warmth.

## Why this relationship matters
This is one of the strongest bridges between the cosmic layer and human layer of the world. The character may possess or eventually acquire extraordinary agency, but the father relationship is grounded in ordinary human systems: authority, recognition, inheritance, fear, resentment, need for distance, and the inability to simply delete the past.

## Psychological axis
The conflict should not be reduced to "evil father versus rebellious son." The most useful recovered structure is a persistent mutual incompatibility in which political authority and familial authority overlap but are not identical.

Key questions:
- Does Almont want obedience, succession, love, fear, or all of them?
- Does the son reject Almont as father, emperor, or both?
- Is returning to the capital an act of duty, strategy, influence, unresolved attachment, or necessity?
- Does either side understand the other's actual motive?
- What changes after each departure and return?

## Canon warning
The accessible memory strongly suggests that the family branch includes Ryota, while another durable memory refers to Jin as the child/young character. These may be the same character in one project/version, distinct characters, or a memory compression across project generations. Do not resolve this silently.
