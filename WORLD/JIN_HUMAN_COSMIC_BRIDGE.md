# Jin — Human ↔ Cosmic Bridge

## Core problem
The archive contains a cosmic/meta-fictional Jin and a separate family-history branch involving Almont, Arisa, and a child/son whose identity may or may not be Jin. The most important unresolved architectural question is where the human and cosmic layers actually connect.

## Three possible architectures
### A — Direct continuity
The imperial child is a Jin variant. Human trauma, family conflict, political exile, and later cosmic agency belong to one continuous identity.

### B — Parallel continuity
The imperial child and cosmic Jin are distinct. The two branches may still mirror one another thematically but do not share biography.

### C — Iterative continuity
An earlier project generation embeds Jin in the imperial branch, while a later generation abstracts him into a more cosmic/meta-fictional role. Both versions are historically meaningful, but they belong to different canonical states.

## Why this matters
The answer changes the meaning of:
- Almont;
- Arisa's death;
- Ryota;
- Adam's loyalty;
- the empire;
- Jin's reality control;
- Jin's relationship to choice;
- the boundary between fictional and human reality.

## Emotional consequence of direct continuity
If the direct-continuity model becomes canonical, one especially powerful structural possibility emerges: a being associated with extraordinary control over reality may originate from an event where, as a child, he could not prevent the death that reshaped his life.

This is **an analytical possibility, not current canon**. It must not be written into the story archive until the identity conflict is resolved.

## Political consequence
If Jin is connected to the imperial family, then the empire is not merely scenery around a cosmic story. Its institutions, succession, family trauma, and rebellion become part of the causal chain that produced or shaped a cosmological actor.

## Adam consequence
If Adam later appears after the human-to-cosmic transition, his relationship to Jin can become evidence about identity continuity. If Adam enters only after the cosmic branch exists, he may instead be independent of the imperial history.

## Garthin consequence
A canonical Garthin/Jin relationship would provide another test: does Garthin know Jin as the child/person from an imperial history, or as a cosmic entity, or both? Current memory does not establish the relationship.

## Evidence required
Look for explicit user statements or scenes connecting:
- Jin to Almont;
- Jin to Arisa;
- Jin to Ryota;
- Jin to the imperial capital;
- Jin's childhood to cosmic abilities;
- Adam to the imperial branch;
- Garthin to Jin;
- the empire to the broader cosmology.

## Resolution rule
Until such evidence exists, keep the human and cosmic layers linked through hypotheses rather than canon.
