# Ryota — Return to the Capital

## Recovered event
Ryota's later return to the capital is remembered as consequential because his position and future ability to influence others make permanent disappearance difficult.

## Why the return matters
The return should not be reduced to a simple reversal of rebellion. It creates a paradox:

**Leaving the capital is an assertion of distance; returning to it is an acknowledgment that distance does not erase political identity.**

## Questions that define the scene
- Who demanded or expected the return?
- Did Ryota choose it freely?
- What had changed while he was away?
- Was his influence already feared?
- Was he returning to challenge Almont?
- Did he return because of duty, strategy, attachment, necessity, or a combination?
- Did the court see the return as victory, threat, or both?
- Was Almont waiting for him?

## Psychological importance
The capital can function simultaneously as:
- home;
- prison;
- political necessity;
- site of paternal authority;
- source of public identity;
- place where the past remains physically present.

These are interpretive dimensions, not hidden canon.
