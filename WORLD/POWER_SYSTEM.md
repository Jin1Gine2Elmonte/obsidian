# Power System — Magic, Powers, Spiritual Vessel

## Core distinction
The remembered worldbuilding distinguishes two major classes of supernatural capability:

### Magic
- beyond ordinary logic;
- difficult to learn;
- operates in a category the user described as beyond logic rather than simply as another school of combat techniques.

### Powers
- more logical/limited than Magic;
- often inherited through blood;
- connected to family lineage and therefore potentially to identity, inheritance, politics, and status.

A spiritual-vessel concept also exists within the worldbuilding.

## Architectural importance
This is not only a combat system. The blood-inheritance dimension creates a bridge between:
- biology/family;
- political succession;
- social hierarchy;
- personal identity;
- historical legacy;
- fear and obligation.

Magic, by contrast, can represent access to a domain not reducible to inherited lineage.

## Questions to recover
- Exact definition of Magic.
- What makes something Magic instead of a Power?
- Can a person learn Magic without inheritance?
- Can Powers be stolen/transferred/suppressed?
- What exactly is a spiritual vessel?
- Is the vessel physical, spiritual, symbolic, or metaphysical?
- Does every powerful person have one?
- Can the vessel be damaged or replaced?
- How does the empire regulate users?
- Are military cities organized around particular Powers?
- Are certain bloodlines politically dominant?
- Can Magic override inherited Powers?
- What are the limits and costs of each?

## Canon protection
Only the broad distinction and blood-inheritance relationship are currently safe to treat as durable memory. Detailed mechanics should be recovered from source conversations before being promoted to canon.
