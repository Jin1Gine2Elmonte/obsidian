# Imperial Scale — Logical Consequences and Recovery Targets

## Declared scale
The empire has been described as controlling the world outside the Dark Forest, with approximately 1,060 military cities and city populations that can reach hundreds of millions within the fictional scale.

## What the scale forces the world to answer
A civilization at this scale cannot operate solely through the image of one emperor issuing commands. It needs intermediate systems. The following are therefore high-priority worldbuilding questions:

### Communication
- How does information cross enormous distances?
- How quickly can Almont receive news?
- What is considered "current" information?
- Can military cities communicate without the capital?

### Supply
- food;
- water;
- weapons;
- medicine;
- manpower;
- transportation;
- reserve stockpiles;
- emergency supply routes.

### Governance
- who governs each city;
- who audits governors;
- how disputes are escalated;
- how taxes/resources are moved;
- how rebellion is detected;
- how local customs interact with imperial law.

### Military
- command layers;
- deployment times;
- garrison sizes;
- specialization by city;
- rapid response;
- border defense;
- Dark Forest containment.

## Important archival distinction
These are not recovered facts from the user's conversations. They are **necessary consistency questions generated by the already-established scale**. They should remain tagged as derived/analysis until the original worldbuilding confirms the actual answers.

## Why this node matters
The empire's scale is one of the places where numerical worldbuilding can become structural worldbuilding. Large numbers are useful only when they generate believable institutions, pressure, and consequences.
