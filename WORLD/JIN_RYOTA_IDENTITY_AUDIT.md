# Jin ↔ Ryota Identity Audit

## Why this audit exists
The current archive contains two durable memory statements that are close enough to be dangerous when merged:

1. A child/young **Jin** is remembered as witnessing Arisa's death, fleeing, returning to the capital, rebelling against his father, and repeatedly leaving the capital.
2. **Ryota** is remembered as Almont's son with a closely related history: witnessing Arisa's death, rebellion against his father, departures, and eventual return because of his future political influence.

The archive must not silently declare these to be the same person.

## Hypotheses

### H1 — Jin and Ryota are the same character under different names/iterations
Evidence required:
- a source explicitly equating the names;
- a transition explaining the name change;
- shared biography in one project/version.

### H2 — Jin and Ryota are distinct characters whose histories were compressed together in a summary
Evidence required:
- any conversation where both appear simultaneously;
- incompatible ages/events;
- distinct relationships to Almont.

### H3 — They belong to different project generations
Evidence required:
- dates/phase markers;
- different worldbuilding contexts;
- later rewrite replacing one identity with another.

### H4 — The current summarized memory contains a naming contamination that cannot be resolved without raw transcripts
This is currently the safest operational position until source-level evidence appears.

## What must NOT happen
- Do not rename one character to the other.
- Do not merge their memories.
- Do not create a single chronology combining both.
- Do not infer identity from similar trauma alone.
- Do not treat a summary's wording as stronger evidence than the original conversation.

## Highest-value recovery queries
Search historical conversations around:
- "Jin" + "Almont";
- "Ryota" + "Almont";
- "Arisa" + "Jin";
- "Arisa" + "Ryota";
- "son" + "Almont";
- "father" + "Jin";
- "father" + "Ryota";
- "capital" + "Jin";
- "capital" + "Ryota";
- escape/return sequences;
- childhood history;
- version/rewrite references.

## Resolution standard
One explicit scene is enough to split a false merge. One explicit user statement equating the names is enough to merge them for a specific project/version. Anything less should remain unresolved.
