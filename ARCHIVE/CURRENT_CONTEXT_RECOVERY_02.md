# I. Story-development workflow and project stages

- The user has repeatedly developed fiction in stages rather than as a single uninterrupted draft: premise -> worldbuilding -> section/chapter drafting -> revision -> visual development -> structural diagnosis.
- One remembered main-novel planning pass targeted roughly 20 chapters.
- One remembered drafting stage had completed sections 1–8 and progressed into section 9.
- A separate medieval-fantasy side-project direction also targeted roughly 20 chapters; this should remain project-specific until its exact identity is recovered.
- The user distinguishes between knowing a story intuitively and being able to execute every part of it in practice. This created an explicit interest in diagnosing craft gaps rather than assuming that conceptual intuition automatically equals finished prose.
- A major craft diagnosis identified an imbalance: mystery/depth could improve while clarity, pacing, movement, and scene progression remained weaker. This became a recurring reason to examine scenes structurally instead of simply making them more mysterious.
- The user has repeatedly asked for chapter/section work to preserve the larger architecture rather than treating each section as an isolated piece.

# II. Fiction-generation and revision patterns

- The user often begins from an abstract or metaphysical nucleus and then expects the story to crystallize through characters, scenes, behavior, and world consequences.
- The user strongly rejects direct moral lecturing when a scene can communicate the same idea through behavior and contradiction.
- Character contradiction is usually intended to remain active rather than being resolved into a single moral label.
- A later reveal of kindness should not automatically function as redemption; it can instead expose another force inside the same character.
- Mystery is valuable when it generates curiosity and interpretive pressure, but it should not replace causality or emotional progression.
- Large metaphysical ideas are expected to acquire scene-level consequences rather than remaining decorative lore.
- The user has repeatedly preferred maintaining tension between enormous scale and intimate emotional stakes.

# III. Specific creative/world details recovered from broader context

## Jin / meta-fictional structure
- Imagination is repeatedly treated as something that can function like a realm or ontological layer rather than merely a mental activity.
- Jin's fictional awareness is a major premise in some versions.
- Reality manipulation is remembered as a consequence of this relationship to fiction/reality, but its exact mechanics are version-specific.
- Jin can occupy multiple narrative roles depending on the project: protagonist, observer, creator-like force, world-traveler, bridge between settings, or cosmological actor.
- The same name can survive while biography, powers, ontology, or project membership changes; this is precisely why the archive uses a version lattice rather than one universal profile.

## Almont / family branch
- Almont's role combines emperor, sovereign, and father, so a family confrontation may simultaneously function as a political confrontation.
- The son is remembered as repeatedly leaving the capital and later returning because his future importance makes his position politically consequential even when he rejects the family environment.
- Return does not equal reconciliation; repeated departures can accumulate meaning rather than simply repeating the same plot beat.
- Benjamin is remembered as a close political advisor whose exact private motives remain under-recovered.
- Arisa should be reconstructed as a person with her own motives and behavior rather than merely as the event that changes the son.

## Empire and world scale
- The imperial world has repeatedly been described as extraordinarily large, including a remembered figure of 1,060 military cities.
- Some remembered population figures reach hundreds of millions in individual cities.
- The user intentionally allows extreme scale in the fiction; the archive therefore must not "correct" the numbers simply because they appear unrealistic.
- Institutional consequences such as supply, taxation, communications, and administration are valid analytical questions but must remain derived unless the source establishes them.

## Dark Forest
- The Dark Forest functions as a structural boundary against total imperial control.
- Its uncertainty is useful: the exact reason imperial power fails there remains a recovery target rather than a blank to fill by generic fantasy logic.
