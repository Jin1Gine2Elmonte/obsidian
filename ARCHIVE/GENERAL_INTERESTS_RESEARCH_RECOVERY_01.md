# General Interests & Research Recovery — Pass 01

## Purpose
Preserve recurring intellectual interests, research/reading preferences, and knowledge-seeking patterns that have appeared across conversations but did not have a dedicated owner in the archive.

This is a preference/context record, not a biography and not a transcript.

## Recurring knowledge domains
The strongest recurring areas of interest include:
- AI and technology;
- literature and writing;
- philosophy and psychology;
- politics and geopolitics;
- creative tools and emerging AI capabilities;
- systems, agents, memory, and model behavior.

## Reading preference
A recurring preference is for substantial long-form reading rather than short news fragments. A useful target format is approximately 25–45 minutes of reading, with strong preference for free access or a legitimate free-access route when available.

A recurring weekly reading goal has also been expressed: find a strong long-form piece published within roughly the previous two weeks and explain why it is worth the reader's time.

## Research style
The user tends to value:
- deep investigation over shallow summaries;
- explanations that preserve uncertainty when evidence is incomplete;
- multiple hypotheses when diagnosing technical or conceptual problems;
- connections across domains when they illuminate structure, but not when they create unsupported continuity;
- primary or high-quality source material when reconstructing history;
- distinctions between observation, inference, interpretation, and speculation.

## Research-to-creation relationship
Research is often used as raw material for creative or technical construction rather than as an end in itself. Useful research should be capable of feeding:
- worldbuilding;
- psychological characterization;
- philosophical depth;
- technical architecture;
- AI workflow design;
- naming and conceptual framing.

## Naming / language interest
Naming and terminology recur as a meaningful part of projects. The archive should preserve:
- original name forms;
- alternate spellings/transliterations when known;
- translation choices;
- semantic meaning;
- phonetic/visual identity;
- project or version membership.

A translated or romanized form should not silently replace the original identity.

## Boundaries
This record intentionally does not encode sensitive personal details, medical information, or unrelated private life context. It captures only durable project-relevant knowledge interests and working preferences.

## Evidence boundary
Most entries are reconstructed from current accessible context rather than verbatim conversation transcripts. Future source recovery may refine, date, split, or supersede them.
