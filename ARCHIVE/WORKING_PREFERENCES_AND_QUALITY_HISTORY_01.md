# Working Preferences & Quality History — Recovery 01

## Purpose
Preserve the recurring preferences, quality criteria, and working expectations that repeatedly shaped the user's collaboration with AI across creative, technical, research, and archival work.

This is a historical recovery layer. It is not a psychological profile, a private biography, or a canonical replacement for the current reasoning/creative-method files.

## Preference for depth over superficial completeness
A recurring requirement is that an answer should not merely be long. It should expose useful structure, causal relationships, hidden constraints, trade-offs, and consequences. Length without new knowledge is explicitly treated as low-value.

### Consequence
When expanding an archive, new content should be preferred over new wrappers. When analyzing a problem, deeper mechanism should be preferred over decorative explanation.

## Purpose before surface
Before generating a large artifact, determine what the artifact is for, what effect it should produce, what its center of gravity is, and what constraints must survive the process.

### Historical correction
A recurring failure mode was replacing the requested concrete task with a more elaborate framework because the framework looked sophisticated.

### Durable implication
Abstraction is subordinate to purpose. A framework is justified only when it improves the requested outcome.

## Preservation of the larger whole
The user repeatedly values continuity across sections, projects, versions, and domains. A local improvement is not successful if it damages the larger structure that gives it meaning.

### Examples of the pattern
- a scene must belong to the trajectory of the story, not merely read well in isolation;
- a visual edit must preserve unrelated approved composition;
- a technical component must respect the source-of-truth architecture;
- a memory addition must have a clear owner and relationship to existing knowledge.

## Sensitivity to micro-details
Small details are often treated as important when they change identity, atmosphere, continuity, causality, or interpretation.

This created a repeated distinction between:
- incidental detail;
- high-signal detail;
- anchor-level detail.

The archive should not flatten these into one undifferentiated memory mass.

## Preference for contradiction over premature resolution
The user repeatedly favors complex states that remain alive when the evidence supports them: contradictory characters, unresolved identity questions, ambiguous project membership, competing technical hypotheses, and incomplete cosmological relationships.

### Durable implication
Do not resolve a contradiction merely to make the archive cleaner. Preserve it when the contradiction itself contains historical or structural information.

## Anti-simplification principle
The user repeatedly rejects reductions that compress a richer structure into an easy label.

Examples include:
- complex character behavior reduced to good/evil;
- a recurring name treated as one immutable identity;
- a shared theme treated as proof of shared universe;
- a model failure treated as having one obvious cause;
- a large world treated as a decorative number without institutional consequences.

## Generation vs evaluation
Creation and criticism are expected to remain distinct passes. Exploration should have room to discover unusual possibilities; evaluation should later test those possibilities against purpose, structure, effect, continuity, and evidence.

This distinction recurs in fiction, image prompting, system architecture, and archive design.

## Search / research style
When research matters, the preferred mode is deep investigation rather than the first plausible answer. Stronger sources and multiple hypotheses should be used when uncertainty can materially affect the result.

The goal is not maximal citation count. The goal is a more accurate underlying model.

## Model independence
A recurring preference in technical work is to preserve knowledge and working intent independently of whichever model, application, gateway, or provider happens to be used at a particular moment.

This is one of the bridges between the creative archive and the technical memory architecture.

## Error inheritance should be intentional
A correction is more valuable when it changes future behavior rather than merely fixing one output.

Therefore durable memory should preserve:
- the failed assumption;
- the observed failure;
- the correction;
- the resulting rule;
- the scope where that rule applies.

## Quality hierarchy
When judging whether a change is valuable, prioritize roughly:
1. new factual knowledge;
2. a meaningful relationship or causal link;
3. a state/history transition;
4. a decision with reason;
5. a constraint or invariant;
6. evidence/provenance;
7. a consequence or testable implication;
8. a resolved contradiction;
9. a useful open question.

More words alone do not increase quality.

## Historical caution
These preferences should not be treated as timeless axioms simply because they are present today. Their strongest status comes from repeated correction, recurrence across domains, and observed practical usefulness.

When stronger historical evidence becomes available, preserve the earlier formulation and record the transition instead of silently rewriting it.
