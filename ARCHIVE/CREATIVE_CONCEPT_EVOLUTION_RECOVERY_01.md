# Creative Concept Evolution Recovery — Pass 01

## Purpose
Preserve the historical movement of recurring creative concepts across projects without collapsing them into one canon.

This node records **concept evolution and recurrence**, not fictional continuity.

## Evidence boundary
The current memory contains strong evidence that certain conceptual axes recur across the user's work, but it does not always preserve the exact first appearance or the complete transition history between projects. Therefore entries below distinguish durable pattern from exact genealogy.

## Recurring axes currently recoverable

### Existence / absence
A recurring concern involving what can exist, what cannot exist, and the paradox produced when an apparently absent principle is given agency or manifestation.

Related recoverable anchors include Entity of Forgetting and broader existence/creation questions. Exact project-by-project genealogy remains incomplete.

### Memory / forgetting
Appears both as a creative/philosophical axis and as a technical systems concern. In fiction it can become identity, loss, erasure, persistence, or ontological paradox; in AI systems it became external memory, provenance, retrieval, and continuity.

The thematic recurrence does **not** itself prove that the fictional Entity of Forgetting and the technical memory system share an in-world relationship.

### Identity / copies / self-definition
Strongly represented in the Jin material through versions, variants, continuity questions, and the unresolved Jin/Ryota identity conflict. Similar identity questions may recur elsewhere without implying the same character or universe.

### Authority / rebellion
Strongly represented in the Almont/Ryota branch and also as a broader creative axis. The retained form is not a simple hero-villain opposition; institutional power and personal resistance accumulate history through repeated interactions.

### Violence / tenderness
A recurring method for constructing psychologically contradictory characters. Garthin is the clearest preserved example: tenderness does not erase brutality, and brutality does not erase the possibility of private tenderness.

### Reality / fiction
Strongly associated with Jin's metafictional/cosmic direction and the broader interest in worlds that may be imagined yet causally meaningful. Exact manifestations differ by project.

### Knowledge / mystery
A central craft problem: mystery has value when it produces productive uncertainty and pressure, but can damage readability and pacing when it outruns orientation and consequence.

### Creation / erasure
Recurs through creation mythology, destruction, forgetting, rewriting, and project evolution. The exact historical lineage between these motifs is incomplete.

## Concept-transition hypotheses (not canon)

These are working historical hypotheses only:

1. Questions about identity and reality appear to have influenced both fictional metafiction and technical thinking about persistent memory.
2. Memory/forgetting became a bridge concept between creative metaphysics and AI architecture, but the two domains must remain separate unless a source explicitly connects them.
3. The user's repeated rejection of moral simplification appears to reinforce a broader preference for systems in which opposing forces coexist rather than cancel one another.
4. Mystery evolved from a primarily atmospheric/aesthetic preference into a craft constraint that must be balanced against progression, causal clarity, and emotional movement.

## Recovery targets
- First known appearance of each major axis.
- Project-by-project manifestations.
- Explicit moments where the user reused a concept intentionally.
- Cases where a concept was renamed or reinterpreted.
- Cases where a concept was abandoned and later revived.
- Distinction between user's deliberate conceptual reuse and accidental thematic similarity.

## Canon protection
This file must never be used to infer:
- shared universe membership;
- shared character identity;
- shared cosmological hierarchy;
- technical/fictional causal links;
- chronology of an idea without evidence.
