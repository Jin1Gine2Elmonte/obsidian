## Meta-fictional boundary
A major recurring premise is that imagination can function as a realm without ordinary physical existence while still affecting humans. Jin is associated with fictional awareness and extraordinary reality agency.

Canonical Jin detail: `WORLD/JIN_CORE.md`.

## Project-boundary rule
The same name, metaphor, or philosophical problem appearing across projects does not establish continuity. Project generation must be identified before edges are promoted to canonical relationships.

## Evidence boundary
Strongly preserved anchor names are not the same thing as a fully recovered hierarchy. The archive should preserve:
- confirmed statements;
- working interpretations;
- project-scoped definitions;
- unknown relationships;
- unsupported hypotheses.

## Writing consequence principle
A cosmological concept becomes narratively valuable when it produces consequences in lower layers—character behavior, institutions, history, powers, geography, language, perception, or decisions. This is a craft heuristic, not automatic canon.

## Recovery priority
Highest-value missing cosmology evidence:
1. explicit hierarchy;
2. project membership;
3. direct definitions by the user;
4. relationships between anchors;
5. concrete scenes/events where cosmological concepts affect the world;
6. rules and consequences;
7. historical changes between project generations.
